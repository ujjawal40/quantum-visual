An atom changing energy level

Bohr said the electron jumps from one level to the next in an instant. The Schrödinger equation shows what really happens: the cloud melts smoothly from one shape into the other, and while it is between the two it sloshes back and forth, and that sloshing is what makes light.

Between two levels

A hydrogen atom in its ground state has its electron in the 1s orbital, a ball. One level up, in 2pz, the cloud is a dumbbell along z. While the atom is changing level, the electron is in a superposition of both:

ψ = c1 ψ1s e−iE1t/ħ + c2 ψ2p e−iE2t/ħ |c₁|² and |c₂|² are the chances of finding the atom in each level. They always add up to 1.

Square it to get where the electron is likely to be. Besides the two clouds, there is a cross term:

|ψ|2 = |c1|2ψ1s2 + |c2|2ψ2p2 + 2|c1||c2| ψ1sψ2p cos(ωt − φ) with ω = (E₂ − E₁)/ħ. The cross term is positive above the nucleus and negative below, so it pushes the cloud up, then down, at frequency ω.

The cloud carries the electron's charge, so a cloud swinging up and down is a tiny antenna. It radiates, or absorbs, light of exactly the frequency it swings at:

f = ω2π = E2 − E1h That is Bohr's rule hf = ΔE, no longer a postulate but a result. For 1s ↔ 2p, ΔE = 10.20 eV and f = 2.47 × 10¹⁵ Hz. Derivation →

The yellow arrow in the simulation is the dipole ⟨z⟩, how far the centre of the cloud sits above or below the nucleus. In a pure level it is zero. Only in a superposition does it swing.

Light drives the jump: Rabi oscillation

Switch on the light. Its electric field pushes the cloud up and down at the light's frequency. If that matches the cloud's own frequency, every push lands in step, and the atom climbs steadily into the upper level. Keep the light on and it is driven back down again, then up, forever. The chance of being in the upper level is

P2(t) = Ω2Ω2 + Δ2 sin2(√(Ω² + Δ²) · t2) Ω, the Rabi frequency, grows with the light's strength. Δ is the detuning: how far the light's frequency is from ΔE/h. Derivation →

On resonance (Δ = 0) the atom goes all the way up. Detune the light and the pushes drift out of step: the atom only gets partway, then falls back sooner. This is how lasers, atomic clocks and quantum computers control atoms, one pulse at a time.

Selection rules: jumps light can't make

How hard the light can push depends on the dipole integral ⟨1|z|2⟩ = ∫ψ₁ z ψ₂ dV, how lopsided the cross term is. Choose 1s ↔ 2s: both clouds are perfect spheres, the cross term is a sphere too, it can only breathe in and out, and ⟨z⟩ = 0. The light finds nothing to push. The rule for one photon is Δl = ±1: the orbital shape letter must change by one step, s ↔ p or p ↔ d. Why →

Because a 2s atom can't drop to 1s by giving out one photon, it lives about 0.12 s, some 75 million times longer than 2p.

Falling back: spontaneous emission

An excited atom left alone does not stay up. Even in empty space it falls, giving out one photon, after 1.6 ns on average for 2p. The fall happens at a random moment, like a nucleus decaying, and the photon flies off as an expanding shell. Look at its shape: bright around the waist and dark along the z axis, because an antenna swinging along z sends no light along its own length.

What is slowed down

The real cloud swings 2.47 × 10¹⁵ times a second, far too fast to see, so here it swings about once a second. The Rabi flip and the fall are slowed too. Everything else is real: the orbitals are the true hydrogen wavefunctions, the dipoles are calculated from them, and the jumps obey the equations above. The light for 1s ↔ 2p is ultraviolet (about 122 nm) and is drawn violet; 2p ↔ 3d is hydrogen's red line at 656 nm.

Bohr vs Schrödinger

What Bohr's model says

The electron sits on one orbit, then leaps to another in no time at all, and a photon appears. The model says nothing about what happens during the leap, or why some leaps never happen.

What actually happens

The amplitudes c₁ and c₂ change smoothly, and the cloud morphs and sloshes as they do. Only when you check which level the atom is in do you get one answer or the other. Forbidden jumps have a zero dipole.

Where they meet: Bohr's energies En = −13.6 eV/n² and his rule hf = E₂ − E₁ both come out of the Schrödinger equation exactly.
