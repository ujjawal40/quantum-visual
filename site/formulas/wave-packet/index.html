The Schrödinger equation, live

This is not an animation someone drew. Your browser is solving the Schrödinger equation on a grid of 16,000 points, many times a second. Send a particle at a wall, through two slits or into a bowl, and watch what the equation does.

The equation being solved

iħ ∂ψ∂t = − ħ22m ∇2ψ + Vψ Left: how ψ changes in time. Right: the kinetic energy term, which makes waves spread and move, plus the potential energy V(x, y) of the landscape.

ψ (psi) is the wavefunction. It is a complex number at every point, so it has a size and a phase, an angle that turns as the wave moves. In the simulation:

  • Height is |ψ|², the probability of finding the particle there. The total volume under the surface is always 1: the particle is somewhere.
  • Colour is the phase. Stripes of colour moving across the packet are its wavelength; the faster the stripes, the faster the particle.
  • The grey floor is the potential V. Raised parts are walls and bowls the particle has to climb.

Everything uses units where ħ = m = 1. A packet with wavenumber k then moves at speed k, and its kinetic energy is k²/2. How the equation is built →

How the computer solves it

Each time step does two exact things in turn. The potential just turns the phase at each point: ψ → e−iVΔt/ħψ. The kinetic term is simple in terms of wavelengths, so the program splits ψ into plane waves with a fast Fourier transform, turns each one's phase by e−iħk²Δt/2m, and adds them back up. Alternating the two, with half a potential step on each side, is called the split-step Fourier method. Every factor has size exactly 1, so total probability cannot leak: watch the readout stay at 100% until the wave reaches the edge of the grid, where a soft absorbing border swallows it.

Four landscapes

Free space: the packet spreads

A particle with a well-defined position must have an uncertain momentum, so its parts move at slightly different speeds and the packet widens. The width grows as

σ(t) = σ0 √(1 + (ħt / 2mσ02)2) A narrow packet (small σ₀) spreads fastest. Try the narrowest width. Derivation →

A barrier: tunnelling

Give the barrier a height V₀ above the packet's energy. A ball would bounce back every time. The wave bounces back mostly, but a part leaks through, because ψ does not stop at the wall: inside it dies away like e−κx, with κ = √(2m(V₀ − E))/ħ. If the wall is thin, some of it is still left on the far side. Double the thickness and the transmitted part drops roughly by a factor of e2κa. The transmission formula →

Two slits: interference

The packet passes through both slits at once. Behind the wall the two halves overlap; where their phases match they add, where they are opposite they cancel. Look at the colours: fringes appear exactly where the stripes from the two slits fall in and out of step.

A harmonic well: a quantum pendulum

In the bowl V = ½mω²r², a packet released from the side swings across and back with period 2π/ω, just like a pendulum, and the right width of packet keeps its shape forever. This is the quantum harmonic oscillator, the model behind molecular vibrations and light itself. Its energy levels →

Measuring collapses the wave

Press Measure position. The program picks one point at random, weighted by |ψ|², exactly as nature does (the Born rule). Then ψ is replaced by a narrow spike at that point. The spread-out wave is gone; immediately afterwards the spike starts to spread again, faster than before, because a sharp position means a very uncertain momentum. The Born rule →

Newton vs quantum

What Newton predicts

A ball with less energy than a wall's height always bounces back. A ball through two slits goes through one of them. A ball in a bowl has a definite position at every instant.

What actually happens

Part of the wave tunnels through the wall. The wave goes through both slits and interferes. Only a measurement gives the particle a definite place, and only for a moment.

Where they meet: the centre of the packet, ⟨x⟩, moves exactly like Newton's ball (Ehrenfest's theorem). In free space it moves at constant speed; in the bowl it swings with period 2π/ω.
