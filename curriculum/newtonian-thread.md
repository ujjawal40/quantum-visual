# The Newtonian thread

Students already know Newtonian mechanics, so every quantum idea is introduced as an answer to a question Newton cannot answer, and every module shows where the two agree. Three devices carry this: a Newton review (lesson 0.4), a "Newton vs quantum" box in every lesson, and paired classical/quantum Python labs.

## Concept by concept

| Idea | Newtonian picture | Quantum picture | Where they meet | Module |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atom | Mini solar system; Coulomb replaces gravity | Probability cloud, no path | Bohr = Newton's mv²/r = ke²/r² + one quantum rule; large-n orbits look classical | 1, 3, 5 |
| Energy | Any value, continuous | Only certain levels: Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV | Level gaps shrink as n grows (correspondence principle) | 3 |
| Electron speed | v = √(ke²/mr) from force balance | Same number in Bohr (2.2 × 10⁶ m/s); only an average in the full theory | Newton's formula gives the right answer | 3 |
| Light | Wave; energy depends on brightness | Photons; energy depends on frequency, E = hf | Many photons behave like a classical wave | 2 |
| Particle motion | Exact position and velocity | Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 | For a cricket ball the uncertainty is unmeasurable | 4 |
| Momentum | p = mv | p = h/λ, also for light | Both hold; a ball's λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m | 4 |
| Angular momentum | Any value | Quantised in units of ħ; spin only ±½ | Large values look continuous | 5 |
| Barriers | Not enough energy → always bounces back | Can tunnel through | Probability falls exponentially with mass and width | 7 |
| Average motion | Newton's second law | Ehrenfest: averages obey F = ma | The quantum average follows Newton | 7 |
| Radiation | Orbiting charge spirals in within ~10⁻¹¹ s | Stationary states do not radiate | The classical failure that motivates the course | 1 |

## Paired "Newton vs quantum" labs

- **Module 1:** simulate a classical electron spiralling into the nucleus (Larmor radiation) and time the collapse.
- **Module 2:** Rayleigh–Jeans vs Planck; watch the classical curve blow up in the ultraviolet.
- **Module 3:** derive Bohr's radius and speed from Newton's force balance plus L = nħ; plot level spacing vs n.
- **Module 4:** de Broglie wavelength of an electron, a dust grain and a car on one log chart.
- **Module 5:** classical vs actual Stern–Gerlach result (smear vs two spots).
- **Module 7:** a ball and an electron at the same barrier; a wave packet's average tracking the Newtonian path.

## Rule of thumb: classical or quantum?

Compare the de Broglie wavelength with the size of the system. If λ is much smaller, Newton works; if they are comparable, quantum effects take over. The course closes with a 3D "scale map" from quarks to planets, shaded by where each theory applies.
